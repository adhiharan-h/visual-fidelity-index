How VFI Is Calculated

1
Physical Pixel Density (PPI)
PPI = √(W² + H²) / size
2
Effective PPI (after display scaling)
Eff.PPI = PPI / scale
3
Pixels Per Degree — Angular Resolution
PPD = 2 × dist × Eff.PPI × tan(0.5°)
4
VFI Score (normalised to 60 CPD limit)
VFI = (PPD / 60) × 100
5
Confidence Band (±3 inch distance uncertainty)
σ_PPD = 2 × 3 × Eff.PPI × tan(0.5°)

Reference: Human visual acuity ≈ 60 cycles/degree (optimal conditions). See: Visual Acuity (Wikipedia), ISO 9241-303.

⚠ Caveat: 60 CPD assumes 20/20 vision and optimal lighting. Real-world average is 20–45 CPD. The VFI 100 threshold is a theoretical ideal.

The Science Behind VFI

Why PPI alone is misleading — and what you should measure instead.

👁

The Problem With PPI

PPI (Pixels Per Inch) is a manufacturing spec. It ignores the most important variable: how far away you are.

A billboard has 2–3 PPI and looks perfectly sharp at 100 feet. A phone screen has 460 PPI and looks blurry at 2 feet if your eyes can resolve that detail.

PPI is a vendor metric. PPD is a perceptual reality.

📐

The Angular Resolution Formula

PPD = 2 · D · PPI · tan(0.5°)
VFI = (PPD / 60) × 100

D = viewing distance (inches) · PPI = effective pixel density · 0.5° = half the minimum angular resolution of human vision · 60 = cycles per degree acuity limit.

🧠

The Human Vision Reference

Under optimal conditions, human visual acuity resolves approximately 60 cycles per degree (CPD) — roughly 1/60th of a degree of arc.

When PPD ≥ 60, individual pixels subtend less than 1/60° of visual angle and become invisible regardless of magnification.

⚠ Real-world average acuity is 20–45 CPD depending on age, lighting, and eye health. VFI 100 is the theoretical limit; VFI 75+ is excellent for most people.

📊

Why a 0–100 Scale?

PPD is technically unbounded and hard to reason about. Normalising to 100 (the human acuity limit) creates an intuitive scale analogous to SPF ratings or fuel economy MPG.

  • VFI 100 = matches acuity limit exactly
  • VFI > 100 = exceeds average human acuity
  • VFI 75 = perceptually excellent for most
  • VFI < 50 = visible pixel structure
⚠

Limitations of This Model

  • Static geometry only — ignores subpixel rendering, anti-aliasing
  • Single acuity threshold — varies significantly by individual
  • No refresh rate — 240Hz vs 60Hz affects motion clarity
  • No colour accuracy — sRGB, P3, Rec.2020 not modelled

VFI intentionally answers one question precisely: "Can I see pixels?" It does not attempt to be a holistic display quality metric.

🔬

Open Methodology

VFI is an open standard. The formula, thresholds, and rationale are fully published. We invite peer review, criticism, and improvement.

If you find an error in our methodology or have research supporting different thresholds, open an issue on GitHub.
